# 🎣 Phishing Awareness Program: Defending the Human Firewall

Welcome to the **Phishing Awareness Training Module**. Despite advancements in firewalls and antivirus software, human users remain the most targeted vulnerability in cybersecurity. This module is designed to train you to recognize, avoid, and report phishing attacks.

---

## 📖 Module 1: Identifying the Bait (Spotting Fakes)

Cybercriminals go to great lengths to make malicious communications look legitimate. Here is how to spot the cracks in their disguise.

### 1. Suspicious Emails
* **Display Name Spoofing:** The sender's name might say "IT Support," but hovering over or clicking the name reveals an address like `support-ticket@gmail-alerts.com` instead of your company's actual domain.
* **Generic Greetings:** Legitimate organizations usually address you by name. Be wary of "Dear Customer" or "Dear User."
* **Unexpected Attachments:** Invoices, receipts, or shipping documents you didn't request—especially `.zip`, `.exe`, or macro-enabled `.docm` files—are major red flags.

### 2. Fake Login Pages & Fraudulent Websites
* **Check the URL:** Look closely at the domain. Hackers use typosquatting (e.g., `www.micros0ft.com` or `www.paypal-secure-login.com`). 
* **SSL is Not a Guarantee:** A padlock icon (HTTPS) only means the connection is encrypted; it does not mean the site itself is legitimate. Scammers secure their fake sites, too.
* **Poor Design & Functionality:** Fake login pages often have broken links at the bottom (like "Terms of Service" or "Privacy Policy" that lead nowhere) or slightly pixelated logos.

## 🧠 Module 2: The Psychology of a Hack (Social Engineering)

Social engineering is the art of manipulating people so they give up confidential information. Attackers rarely rely on technical hacking; they rely on human emotions.

* **Urgency & Fear:** "Your account will be suspended in 24 hours if you do not verify your details." This forces you to act quickly without thinking.
* **Authority:** An email appearing to be from the CEO demanding an urgent wire transfer to a "new vendor" (known as Business Email Compromise or Whaling).
* **Familiarity & Trust:** Spoofing the identity of a known vendor or partner you frequently do business with.
* **Curiosity:** Sending an email titled "Q3 Salary and Bonus Adjustments.xlsx" to tempt employees into clicking a malicious attachment.

## 📂 Module 3: Real-World Case Studies

Even tech giants and major corporations fall victim to phishing when human verification fails.

### Case Study 1: Google and Facebook ($100 Million Loss)
* **What Happened:** Between 2013 and 2015, a hacker set up a fake company impersonating a legitimate Asian hardware vendor that both Google and Facebook used.
* **The Method:** He sent forged invoices and phishing emails. Because the name was familiar, employees bypassed standard verification protocols.
* **The Result:** The two tech giants wired over $100 million to the attacker's offshore accounts before realizing the fraud.

### Case Study 2: Ubiquiti Networks ($46.7 Million Loss)
* **What Happened:** In 2015, the networking company lost $46.7 million to a targeted spear-phishing attack.
* **The Method:** Attackers impersonated executives and instructed the finance department to make urgent, secretive wire transfers to foreign bank accounts.
* **The Result:** The employees, pressured by "authority" and "urgency," complied without verifying the request over the phone.

## 🛡️ Module 4: Practical Defense & Best Practices

To protect yourself and your organization, adopt the **S.L.A.M.** verification method:

1. **S - Sender:** Always check the actual email address, not just the display name.
2. **L - Links:** Hover over links *without clicking* to preview the true destination URL.
3. **A - Attachments:** Never open unexpected attachments. When in doubt, call the sender to verify.
4. **M - Message:** Look for typos, poor grammar, and urgent/threatening language.

**Additional Security Measures:**
* **Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA):** Even if an attacker steals your password, they cannot access your account without your secondary device.
* **Verify Out-of-Band:** If you receive an email asking for a wire transfer, password change, or sensitive data, verify it through a different channel (e.g., call the person on a known phone number).
* **Report, Don't Just Delete:** If you spot a phishing email at work, report it to your IT/Security department immediately so they can block it for everyone else.

- A) Click the link quickly before the 2 hours run out.
- B) Reply to the email asking if it's real.
- C) Report the email to your actual IT department and do not click the link.

**Q2:** True or False: If a website has a padlock icon next to the URL (HTTPS), it is 100% safe to enter your credit card information.
- A) True
- B) False

**Q3:** What is "Whaling"?
- A) Phishing emails sent randomly to thousands of people.
- B) A highly targeted phishing attack aimed at senior executives (like CEOs or CFOs).
- C) Sending malicious text messages.
